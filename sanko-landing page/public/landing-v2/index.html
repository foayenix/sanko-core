Turn living knowledge into verifiable evidence.

African traditional medicine holds generations of knowledge in practitioners’ memories, in handwritten books, and in daily practice. Very little of it is written down in a form that outlives the practitioner.

Sanko works with what a practitioner already sends on WhatsApp: a voice note in their own language, a typed message, a photograph of a page. Each one becomes a structured record with their name on it, private to their practice.

Every time they use a formulation again, it goes onto the same record. Over years that becomes a documented history of who used what, for whom, and what happened next.

It starts with a voice note.

Practitioners already record what they do: spoken into a phone, written down, or kept in a book they have had for years. Sanko works with all three inside WhatsApp, so there is nothing to install and no new interface to learn.

  1. Capture

    The practitioner sends a voice note in their own language, a typed message, or a photograph of a page. It reaches the Sanko agent as an ordinary WhatsApp message.

  2. Structure

    Sanko pulls out the plants, parts, preparation, doses and indications. Local names resolve to botanical ones, and the local name stays the one on the record.

  3. Follow

    The practitioner says who they treated, with what, and for how long. Sanko asks that patient for consent, then checks in on day three, day seven and onward. The patient replies to the agent.

  4. Accumulate

    Each episode goes onto the same formulation’s record. Where consent allows, that record becomes something a research team can work from.

The agent does the following up

The practitioner hands over three things once: who was treated, with what, and for how long. From there the agent asks the patient’s permission, then checks in on the schedule the practitioner set.

The patient answers in their own words, and every reply goes onto that formulation’s record. Follow-up stops when the course ends, or earlier if the patient recovers or sees the practitioner again.

A documented history takes years to build.

A single record is a note. What makes it worth anything is the hundredth entry against the same formulation, years later, with every source still named. That is the timescale Sanko is built for.

  • A shortlist for researchers

    Documented use does not show that a formulation works. It does show a research team which formulations have been used often enough, and recorded carefully enough, to be worth studying properly.

  • Aggregation needs permission

    Anything aggregated runs on consented, de-identified records. The practitioners behind a formulation stay named on it, and stay in control of it.

  • Worth most in ten years

    A formulation documented today is most useful a decade from now, so the consent, attribution and correction models are the parts being settled first.

The same records, read four ways.

Sanko makes practitioner records legible to an organisation while the practitioner’s name stays attached to them.

  • Traditional medicine organisations

    Strengthen how members document their work, and keep local naming systems in place while doing it.

  • Research teams

    Work from structured records that still carry the practitioner, the language and the context they came from.

  • NGOs and funders

    See what a programme is producing, in aggregate and de-identified.

  • Regulators and public bodies

    Review how knowledge is documented, consented to and attributed, with the limits of that documentation stated on the record.

Every record carries its source.

Attribution and consent are fields on the record, written at the moment of capture. Any later use of that record can be traced back to the practitioner it came from.

  • Run on Sanko’s own machines

    Transcription, page reading and extraction run on models Sanko operates itself, so practitioner and patient content does not have to be handed to a third-party AI service to be read.

  • Scoped to the practitioner

    A practitioner sees their own records and nobody else’s. Patient tracking documents what was reported; it is not a measure of whether a treatment works.

  • Nagoya-aligned

    Records carry the source attribution and provenance that access and benefit-sharing agreements under the Nagoya Protocol depend on. Sanko follows those principles; it does not certify compliance with them.
