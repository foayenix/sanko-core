Where these mappings come from

Every plant name Sanko publishes comes from one of 7 peer-reviewed ethnobotanical surveys of Nigerian flora.

  • Ethnobotanical Studies of Port Harcourt Metropolis, Nigeria

    CC BY 3.0 · 228 mappings published · 10.1155/2013/829424

  • Ethnobotanical Survey of Local Flora Used for Medicinal Purposes among Indigenous People in Five Areas in Lagos State, Nigeria

    CC BY 4.0 · 270 mappings published · 10.3390/plants11050633

  • Ethnobotanical survey of anti-asthmatic plants in South Western Nigeria

    open access; article-specific reuse terms not stated in the PDF · 46 mappings published · 10.4314/ajtcam.v5i4.31288

  • Ethnobotanical survey of medicinal plants used in the treatment of animal diarrhoea in Plateau State, Nigeria

    CC BY 2.0 · 95 mappings published · 10.1186/1746-6148-7-36

  • Ethno medicinal survey of plants used by the indigenes of Rivers State of Nigeria

    free to read at the publisher (bronze open access); no reuse licence stated by the publisher or recorded in Crossref, OpenAlex or Europe PMC · 186 mappings published · 10.3109/13880209.2012.661740

  • Ethnobotanical Survey of Plants Used for Treating Cough Associated with Respiratory Conditions in Ede South Local Government Area of Osun State, Nigeria

    CC BY 4.0 · 98 mappings published · 10.3390/plants9050647

  • Ethnobotanical survey and conservation of the indigenous plants used for traditional orthopedic care practices in Bayelsa Central Senatorial District, Nigeria

    diamond open access; no reuse licence stated in the article, on the journal site, or in Crossref and OpenAlex metadata · 43 mappings published · 10.13057/asianjethnobiol/y050206

Registered but not published

Sanko holds further sources that do not meet its export conditions. They are listed here because an omission that nobody can see is not a provenance claim.

Which Plants for What Ailments: A Quantitative Analysis of Medicinal Ethnobotany of Ile-Ife, Osun State, Southwestern NigeriaquarantinedThe published tables are retained in surveys/quarantine for auditability, but excluded from all generated mappings because the publisher retracted the article.
Ethnobotanical Survey of Medicinal Plants Used in the Treatment of COVID-19 and Related Respiratory Infections in Ogbomosho South and North Local Government Areas, Oyo State, Nigeriastaged_reviewAll 86 rows of Tables 2, 3 and 4 (COVID-19, cough, flu) are preserved, each with its own table locator, but production export is blocked pending botanical review. The local-name column is unlabelled; the methods state that names were collected in Yoruba. Several determinations disagree with the other registered sources for the same Yoruba name: Egbeesi as Neonauclea excelsa (Egbesi is Nauclea latifolia in two surveys), Iyere as Piper nigrum (Piper guineense in two sources), Asofeyeje as Nigella sativa (Rauvolfia vomitoria in two sources), Epo aganwo as Drypetes gossweileri (Oganwo is a Khaya in three sources). The build only detects a disagreement between identical spellings, so including the table as it stands would export some of these as resolved mappings. The COVID-19, cough and flu indications are survey context only.
Indigenous medicinal plants used in folk medicine for malaria treatment in Kwara State, Nigeria: an ethnobotanical studystaged_reviewAll 62 rows are preserved, but production export is blocked pending language review. Only six rows label their names Yoruba or Nupe; the other 56 state no language, interviews were conducted in both, and at least one unlabelled name is Hausa (Karandafi) and one is a nickname (June 12). Unlabelled names are staged with a null language.
Ethnobotanical uses of plants in Nigeria: an analysis of current research trends and patternsdiscovery_onlyTable 2 is retained as a catalogue of 79 original studies to acquire. Table 3 is retained as secondary review evidence only and cannot produce runtime mappings.
Medicinal Plants in Tropical West Africareference_onlyRetained for botanical, synonym, pharmacology, and bibliographic checking. The book does not provide a structured language-labelled vernacular index, so no local-name mappings were inferred from its prose.
Handbook of African Medicinal Plants, Second Editionstaged_reviewOnly concise mapping facts and page locators are staged. Runtime export is blocked because most vernacular labels lack a row-specific country or locality and have not been reviewed by language practitioners.
Europe PMC and Crossref primary-source discoverydiscovery_onlyWritten by scripts/discover-sources.js. A ranked list of Nigerian ethnobotanical sources to acquire, and the resolution of the 79 original studies catalogued in anumudu-2025-nigeria-review to DOIs, licences and full texts. Candidates are citations, not data: no row here produces a vernacular mapping, and an acquired source only becomes ingestable when it is registered in this file in its own right.
Wikidata taxon labels in Nigerian languagesdiscovery_onlyWritten by scripts/import-wikidata-vernaculars.js. Labels are crowd-sourced and unsourced — no locality, no collector, no citation — and a large share of those returned in an African language are the botanical or English name filed in the wrong language. They are candidates for review, never mappings. A candidate reaches the runtime index only through practitioner confirmation, attributed to the reviewer who confirmed it and carrying the candidate source it came from.

Sanko publishes plant name mappings and the parts reported in the source literature. It does not publish therapeutic claims, and nothing here is medical guidance.
