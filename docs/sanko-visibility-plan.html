Sanko

Search & AI-answer strategy · Draft 1 · 11 September 2026

Getting found when your own name belongs to someone else.

Sanko is absent from search because the site was never indexable, and absent from AI answers because the phrase “Sanko Africa” already resolves to a Madagascan insurance broker. This plan treats those as two different problems, because they are.

01

The premise this plan rests on

PRODUCT.md is explicit that the website serves companies, funders, NGOs, research institutions, regulators and traditional-medicine organisations evaluating Sanko. Practitioners — the people who actually use the product — arrive through WhatsApp and word of mouth. They are not searching Google for formulation software.

That single fact rewrites the keyword strategy. Most of the obvious terms (“herbal record keeping app”, “traditional medicine software”) target a user who will never see the site. The institutional queries that do matter are specialised and low-volume — too thin, on their own, to build the authority needed to rank for anything.

The resolution is the plant dataset. It is the only asset Sanko owns that carries real search demand and the citation structure that AI engines reward. It earns the authority; the institutional pages spend it.

02

Layer A — becoming a distinct entity

The AI Overview answering “Sanko Africa” with a Madagascan insurance broker is not a ranking failure. It is an entity resolution failure: no model has a record of Sanko-the-evidence-infrastructure, so it reaches for the nearest confident match. You cannot outrank your way out of this. You establish a separate entity, or you stay merged with theirs.

  1. Delete the 192.64.119.110 parking record at Namecheap. Everything below is wasted effort while half of all requests to the apex hang.
  2. Google Search Console — verify, submit the sitemap, request indexing.
  3. Bing Webmaster Tools — usually skipped, and a mistake to skip. Bing's index is a primary retrieval source for ChatGPT search and Microsoft Copilot. For GEO this is not optional; it is the cheapest single action on this page.
  4. Create a Wikidata item for Sanko, with distinct statements: instance of software/organisation, field of work, country, founding date, official website. Wikidata is a direct input to knowledge panels and a heavily-weighted disambiguation source for language models. This is the highest-leverage GEO action available.
  5. Publish an /about page that says what Sanko is and is not — in plain, extractable sentences. Include an explicit disambiguation line: “Sanko is not affiliated with Sanko Africa, the insurance brokerage in Antananarivo, Madagascar.” Models quote sentences like that verbatim.
  6. Align the entity description everywhere — LinkedIn, GitHub org, Crunchbase, any funder or accelerator listing. Identical wording across independent domains is what converts a claim into a consensus.
  7. Extend the Organization schema already on the homepage with sameAs (every profile above), disambiguatingDescription, foundingDate and areaServed.
  8. Add /llms.txt — a plain-text summary of what the site covers. Cheap, increasingly respected, and nothing is lost if adoption stalls.
Why the name collision is survivable

You are not trying to win the word “Sanko”. You are trying to make sure that when someone asks an assistant “what is sanko.africa” or “who is documenting African traditional medicine with AI”, the answer is yours. Those are winnable. The bare brand term, against an Axian Group subsidiary and an established fashion platform with sitelinks, is not — and does not need to be.

03

Layer B — the plant name resolver

data/plants/ holds 479 local-name entries and 281 botanical records verified against the GBIF taxonomy backbone, each traceable to a named ethnobotanical study down to the supplementary table and row. Nothing comparable exists as structured, English-language, source-attributed web content.

Every mapping is a page. Every page answers a question somebody actually types, and answers it with a citation — which is precisely the shape of content an AI engine will quote rather than paraphrase away.

The ambiguity pages are the differentiator

ambiguities.json records where published sources disagree — abafe resolves to Piliostigma thonningii in the 2022 Lagos survey and P. reticulatum in the 2008 anti-asthmatic study. Most publishers hide that. Publishing the conflict, with both sources named and neither presented as settled, is the single most citable thing on the site: it is genuinely useful, impossible to fabricate, and exactly what a model reaches for when a user asks a question with no clean answer.

Hub pages that collect the long tail

  • Yoruba medicinal plant names, with accepted botanical equivalents — the index page
  • By region — plants attested in Lagos State, Plateau State, Port Harcourt, Ile-Ife
  • By part used — bark, leaf, root, stem-bark
  • By preparation — infusion, decoction, poultice, juice
  • The source bibliography, one page per study, linking every mapping drawn from it

Markup

DefinedTermSet for the collection and DefinedTerm per mapping; Dataset on the hub with citation pointing at each study's DOI. Taxon identifiers give engines something unambiguous to bind to.

Hard boundary

Publish only mappings derived from the peer-reviewed literature. practitioner_confirmations currently reads 0; when it does not, those records must stay unpublished. Practitioner-contributed knowledge behind a Nagoya-aligned consent model cannot become SEO inventory — doing so would contradict the exact governance claim the institutional pages are built on, and it is the exact failure mode your buyers are watching for.

The literature is already public. Structuring, verifying and crediting it is a contribution. Extracting practitioner records would not be.

The constraint that shapes all of this

Traditional medicine is YMYL territory — Google holds health-adjacent content to a much higher evidence bar, and PRODUCT.md already forbids inventing metrics, testimonials, customer logos or clinical claims. Those two constraints point the same way. Write about documentation, governance and provenance, never about what a plant treats. The moment a page reads as herbal health advice it inherits a credibility test Sanko cannot pass and does not want.

05

Writing for AI answers specifically

GEO overlaps with SEO but diverges in ways that change how pages are written. Retrieval systems chunk a page into passages and rank those passages independently of the document. Several habits of good marketing copy actively break that.

  • Name the subject in every claim. “We keep records private” is useless once it is chunked away from the page — the subject is gone. “Sanko processes practitioner records locally” survives retrieval intact. Audit the site for we and our in any sentence carrying a fact.
  • Lead with the answer. A heading phrased as the real question, answered in the first sentence beneath it, then the detail. Retrieval favours self-contained passages.
  • Numbers get cited disproportionately. “479 local plant names mapped to 281 GBIF-verified botanical records across three peer-reviewed Nigerian surveys” is far more quotable than any adjective. Sanko has real figures — use them, and date them.
  • Third-party corroboration outweighs your own site. A model trusts a claim that appears across independent sources. One mention in a journal, an NGO report or a reputable directory is worth more than ten pages you publish yourself.
  • Keep facts in text, not in images or scripts. The live plant count rendered by plant-count.js is invisible to most crawlers — emit the number server-side into the HTML as well.

Checking whether it worked

There is no Search Console for AI answers. Test by hand, monthly, and log it: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google AI Mode “what is sanko.africa?”, “who is building documentation infrastructure for African traditional medicine?” and “what is the Yoruba name for Azadirachta indica?” The first tells you whether the entity has separated from the insurance broker. The third tells you whether Layer B is being cited.

What this plan will not do

It will not make Sanko rank first for “Sanko” — that name is held by older, larger organisations, and no amount of technical work changes it. It will not produce meaningful traffic in the first quarter; a pre-launch domain with no backlinks earns authority slowly and there is no shortcut that is not also a risk.

What it will do is make Sanko findable by the people who matter — the programme officer researching Nagoya-aligned documentation, the researcher checking a Yoruba plant name, the regulator reading about ICD-11 TM2 — and give language models a correct, well-sourced account of what Sanko is when they are asked. For an institutional product with no consumer funnel, that is the entire game.
