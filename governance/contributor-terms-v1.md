# Sanko contributor terms — v1 DRAFT

> **This draft has had no legal review and no practitioner consultation.**
> It is the text the acceptance mechanism points at so the mechanism can be built
> and tested. It must not be presented to a practitioner as binding, quoted to a
> partner as Sanko's position, or treated as advice. Two things have to happen
> before it is any of those: review by a lawyer competent in Nigerian law and the
> Nagoya Protocol, and consultation with the practitioners it governs — who
> should shape it rather than receive it.
>
> Version and hash are what a practitioner's acceptance is recorded against, so
> changing a word here creates a new version they have not accepted.

## What you keep

Your formulations, your patients' records, and your own words remain yours. Sanko
stores them so you can use them; it does not acquire them.

- You can export everything Sanko holds about you at any time.
- You can delete your account, and Sanko deletes what it holds.
- Nobody else — including Sanko's operators — treats your records as their own
  material to publish, sell, or hand on.

## What Sanko may do without asking again

- Store and process your records so the service works.
- Use your corrections to improve the models that read your messages, on
  Sanko's own machines.
- Count your records in aggregate, non-identifying figures — "N formulations
  documented" — with no names, no conditions, no plants, no locations.

## What requires a separate agreement, every time

Any use of what you have documented beyond the above. That includes research
access, licensing, publication, and any dataset that leaves Sanko's own machines.

For each such use, before it happens:

1. you are told who wants it, what for, and what would be included;
2. you decide, and you can say no without losing access to Sanko;
3. what you receive in return is agreed and written down;
4. the use, and what was agreed, is recorded in a ledger you can inspect.

A practitioner who has not accepted these terms cannot be included in any such
use at all. That is enforced in the software, not only promised here.

## Attribution

Where your knowledge is used with your agreement, you are credited in the form
you choose — your name, your practice, or a pseudonym. You can change this.

## Plant name confirmations

Confirming that a local name refers to a particular plant adds that mapping to
Sanko's shared plant index, which every practitioner benefits from. Vernacular
names are community knowledge rather than any individual's, and the mapping
carries the confirming reviewer's reference rather than any practitioner
identity. This is the one contribution that is shared by default; say so if you
would rather a name you use were not.

## Changes

New terms are a new version. Your acceptance applies to the version you accepted
and to nothing else, and Sanko must ask again.
